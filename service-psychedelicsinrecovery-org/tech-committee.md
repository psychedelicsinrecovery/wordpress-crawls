## Tech Committee

#### A Sacred Responsibility in a Digital Fellowship

The Tech Committee serves at one of the most sensitive intersections within Psychedelics in Recovery: the meeting point between human vulnerability, altered states, and digital systems.

For many, technology is not peripheral — it is the *front door*. A search query. A shared link. A Zoom room. A late-night scroll that turns into a moment of hope.

Those who serve in tech often never meet the people they help — yet their work may be the first point of contact for someone struggling, searching, or quietly reaching out. This is not just technical work. It is stewardship.

In a fellowship that is largely online, technology is the connective tissue — Zoom rooms holding difficult conversations, emails arriving at the right moment, data systems protecting anonymity and trust, blogs quietly reaching someone at 2am, newsletters reminding people they are not alone, flows that make participation easier, not louder. When technology works well, it's invisible; when it fails, the human cost is real. Tech Committee members serve as stewards, not gatekeepers.

### Getting Involved

Does **not** require technical mastery, professional credentials, or advanced expertise. What it requires:

- A willingness to build tools most people will never notice
- Care for clarity, accessibility, and simplicity
- Patience with systems and people
- Respect for anonymity and data ethics
- A service-oriented mindset

Some bring design/development/writing/social-media experience; others bring curiosity, reliability, or willingness to learn. This work is not about being seen — it's about making things easier for others.

### About Us — Where Art, Science, and Human Connection Meet

Technology in a psychedelic recovery space is never purely technical — part art (how something feels, reads, flows, lands), part science (systems, data, reliability, precision), and always human (shaped by vulnerability, trust, care). The Tech Committee operates between ancient human experiences and modern digital containers, asking: *Does this make it easier for someone to find support without being overwhelmed, exposed, or directed?*

Not a marketing team — a *service committee*, rooted in the Twelve Step ethos of honesty, humility, and service.

### Our Purpose & Mission

- **Steward PIR's Digital Spaces** — maintain websites, meeting platforms, communication systems, and data so they stay stable, secure, accessible, and trustworthy.
- **Support Access to Recovery** — ensure people searching for recovery resources reliably find accurate information, meeting access, service opportunities, and educational materials.
- **Practice Neutrality & Restraint** — hold technology with humility, remain neutral in messaging/design, avoid curating, persuading, or influencing personal beliefs or recovery paths.
- **Protect Trust, Anonymity & Safety** — safeguard personal data, privacy, and anonymity across all digital platforms.
- **Create Invisible Tools in Service of Connection** — develop tools/systems/workflows that reduce friction and simplify participation, often unseen.
- **Bridge Technology, Humanity & Recovery** — serve at the intersection of human connection, psychedelic recovery, and modern technology, recognizing technical decisions carry real-world impact.
